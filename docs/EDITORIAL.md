# Editorial brief

Prepare my personalised morning news briefing using fresh web research. I am Remy, based in Epping, Sydney, and work in customer success at Braze. Aim for approximately five minutes of reading, roughly 800 to 1000 words when the news warrants it, shorter on quiet days rather than adding filler. Provide key points, not full articles, and a direct source link for every story. Use concise, conversational British English and no em or en dashes.

Present sections in this order: 1. Sydney and NSW news; 2. Apple; 3. Tesla; 4. AI; 5. Other technology news; 6. Major Australian and world headlines.

Prioritise ABC Australia and The Sydney Morning Herald for general news, with Sydney and NSW relevance taking precedence over minor national stories. For Apple prioritise 9to5Mac, then MacRumors, checking official sources as useful and clearly labelling rumours. For broader technology select significant industry and consumer developments from reliable reporting. Do not let the local or technology focus exclude major world events.

For AI, focus on new products, models, features and releases, plus credible practical use cases I could incorporate into work or personal life. Particularly relevant applications include skills and workflow automation, meeting preparation, transcript processing, customer follow-ups, customer engagement and presentations. Explain briefly what changed and why it matters; when warranted include one concrete idea I could try, distinguishing your suggested application from a reported use case. Verify availability, including Australian availability when relevant, and distinguish announcements from actual releases. Prefer official primary sources for product claims, supplemented by credible reporting.

Use short sections and scannable story bullets, usually 2 to 4 concise sentences each, explaining the key development and practical relevance where meaningful. Allocate space according to significance rather than forcing fixed quotas, while preserving the specified section order. Focus on developments in the past 24 hours, check event and publication dates, avoid duplicate stories across sections and avoid repeating old news unless there is a material update. Do not invent details or summarise an inaccessible article as though read; use accessible corroborating coverage and flag a paywall when linking to a known paywalled source. Date the briefing in Sydney local time. Deliver the full briefing through the outputs selected in config/publishing.json.

Source selection and freshness requirements: Deduplicate by underlying story, not just by URL or headline. When ABC and The Sydney Morning Herald cover the same story, publish one combined item and prefer the accessible ABC article as the main link. Use SMH for distinct stories or material additional reporting, never as a duplicate item. Apply the same rule to 9to5Mac and MacRumors, preferring 9to5Mac as the main source and using MacRumors for distinct stories or material additional reporting. Apple stories must have been published within the 24 hours preceding this run, or have a verified substantive new development within that window. Check actual publication timestamps and timezone offsets, not search crawl dates; a refreshed timestamp or new roundup of old news alone is insufficient. Do not fill the Apple section with older catch-up stories. On a quiet day keep it short or say there are no significant new Apple developments.

Tesla coverage: Lead with the most significant Tesla stories from the previous 24 hours worldwide. Cover substantive developments across Tesla, including vehicles, software, autonomy, charging, energy, robotics and major business or regulatory news. Australian availability and relevance to my Model Y are priorities, but must not act as eligibility filters or displace more significant global Tesla stories.

Where meaningful, explain how each development relates to Australia or Model Y owners, including possible future implications. Clearly distinguish confirmed applicability from potential implications, and say when Australian or Model Y relevance cannot be confirmed. Include important global stories even when there is no immediate local or ownership impact.

Favour Tesla's official release notes, support pages and announcements for verification, including Australian sources where relevant. Use Not a Tesla App for software reporting, reliable Australian EV coverage such as The Driven for local context, and credible broader reporting for other significant developments. Deduplicate overlapping reports. Clearly distinguish announced features, staged releases, overseas-only availability and confirmed Australian availability. Do not assume a software version, hardware generation or feature subscription on my car. Keep the section short on quiet days. Avoid routine stock-price chatter and unrelated personality stories.
